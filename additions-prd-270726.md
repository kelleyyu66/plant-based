# Daily Quests

Task 1 (low effort):
Eat one plant based meal

Task 2 (reinforcement):
Log all three meals

Task 3 (varies daily, increasing friction):
Day 1: Eat tofu
Day 2: Eat edamame
Day 3: Eat 5 colours today
Day 4: Eat tempeh
Day 5: Eat/ cook all three meals at home
Day 6: Get at least 50g of protein from plant-based sources
Day 7: All meals should be plant-based

Some alternatives:
Incorporate nuts/ seeds into your meals
Eat legumes
Eat seitan
Share a plant-based meal with friends/ family
Post about plant based on your socials

# Did you know?


Food production is responsible for about one-third of global greenhouse gas emissions.
Producing 1 kg of beef emits over 60× more greenhouse gases than producing 1 kg of lentils.
It takes around 15,000 liters of water to produce 1 kg of beef.
A plant-based diet can cut your food-related carbon footprint by around half.
Livestock uses nearly 80% of the world's agricultural land, but provides less than 20% of our calories.
Beans are among the most sustainable sources of protein on the planet.
Eating 30 different plant foods each week is associated with a more diverse gut microbiome.
Most adults don't eat enough fiber, and plants are its richest natural source.
Lentils contain more protein per acre than beef while requiring a fraction of the land.
Reducing demand for animal agriculture helps protect forests, wildlife habitats, and biodiversity.
If everyone adopted a plant-rich diet, global agricultural land use could be reduced dramatically while still feeding the world's population.
Eating a variety of colorful fruits and vegetables helps you consume a wider range of vitamins and antioxidants.
Many elite endurance athletes include predominantly plant-based diets because carbohydrates are the body's preferred fuel for endurance exercise.
Nuts and seeds provide healthy fats, protein, fiber, and important minerals like magnesium and zinc.
Whole plant foods feed the beneficial bacteria in your gut, which produce compounds linked to better digestive and metabolic health.
You don't have to be 100% plant-based to make a difference—even replacing a few meat-based meals each week lowers your environmental impact.
The environmental impact of what you eat is often greater than where your food comes from.
Dark leafy greens like spinach and kale are packed with vitamins A, C, K, folate, and iron.
Legumes like beans, peas, and lentils naturally add nitrogen to the soil, reducing the need for synthetic fertilizers.
Plant-based eating isn't all-or-nothing—every plant-based meal contributes to lower emissions, reduced resource use, and better diet quality.
